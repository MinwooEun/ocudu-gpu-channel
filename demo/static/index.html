What are these numbers?

One CUDA kernel launch computes ALL channel lanes for one 5G slot: 97 µs measured at 16 edges, against the 500 µs slot budget at 30 kHz subcarrier spacing — i.e. the emulator uses ~19 % of real time and the rest is headroom. 183× is the same workload against the CPU implementation (58,430 µs → 319 µs end-to-end). The telemetry rate is measured live off the broker's ZMQ PUB feed — this page is a consumer of it.

What am I looking at?

Left — measured |hrc|²: the four spatial channel coefficients of a 2×2 MIMO link, measured live by alternating TX pilots (each cell = the power one RX port receives while only one TX port is on air). They fade continuously — Rayleigh mean ≈ 3.47 per lane — because the channel model is TR 38.901 TDL-A with Jakes Doppler at 10 Hz. Middle — the correlation experiment: all TX ports send the same unit-power tone, so each bar's expected height is exactly computable: 6.94 when the antennas fade independently (iid), 9.71 when TX correlation 0.4 is on — correlated channels combine more coherently. The bars are live measurements; the dashed lines are that math. Bars sitting on the line = the GPU emulator is provably correct. Right — the buttons change the running channel through its ZMQ control plane, applied at a slot boundary — no restart, no rebuild. Watch the bars migrate from one dashed line to the other within a few seconds of fading.

What am I looking at?

The green line is total received power breathing under multipath fading — this is what a moving receiver experiences, generated in real time on the GPU. The Doppler is set to 10 Hz (~pedestrian speed) so the envelope is visible to the eye; at highway speeds it would look like noise at this refresh rate. Dashed lines are the analytic iid/correlated levels; amber markers stamp the moment each control action (correlation swap, path loss) was applied — cause and effect on one timeline.

Scaling toward massive MIMO projection anchored at measured 16-edge kernel = 97 µs; the 500 µs line is the SCS-30 slot budget

Nt·Nr lane expansion is quadratic — the research direction is a low-rank decomposition (L spatial components instead of Nt·Nr lanes) to stay under the real-time budget at massive-MIMO scale.
Why this matters

The green bar is measured; the rest are linear-in-lanes projections. A naive scale-up crosses the 500 µs real-time line somewhere before 128 edges — so massive-MIMO-scale emulation cannot be brute-forced even on this GPU. That gap is the research pitch: exploit spatial correlation to emulate L dominant components instead of all Nt·Nr lanes, trading a provable approximation error for staying inside the slot budget.
